Branding and interface

Block Viewer’s interface is clean, modular and slightly glassy: deep surface neutrals, a signal-lime accent, readable text, crisp icons. Every screen must read as Block — not as another viewer’s purple gradient skin.

Palette

The colours come from crates/block-viewer/src/theme.rs (Theme::default). In the code, always use the resolved Palette (p.violet, p.ink…), never a hard-coded colour: a skin can change them, and so can the user, from Preferences › Appearance › Skin › Interface colours (interface_colors.rs): they pick the accent (violet role → signal), the windows’ background (panel) and the text (ink), and the other accent, surface and text roles follow from those three. Field names like violet / indigo are historical role ids; hex values are Block brand tokens.

Design rules

  • The look comes from the theme. A window writes no colour, no radius and no stroke: it takes the roles of the Palette, the radii of the theme and the shared widgets of ui/widgets.rs. What a window draws with its own values is content, not dressing: colours of a map and of its legend, maturity rating colours, inside of a colour swatch, image shown as it is (widgets::PICTURE_TINT). A test checks it (ui/theme_guard.rs, run by check.ps1); a content line is declared there with a comment // theme-guard: content — the reason.
  • Discreet glass. A shared GPU blur appears in the windows, the dialogs, the top and bottom bars and everything laid over the world (notifications, volume, emoji, media controls, local chat bubbles, pie menu), under a dark tint. A soft shadow and a thin border separate the surface from the world; the fields stay calm and readable. A skin with panel_alpha = 255 keeps opaque surfaces without this blur. The high contrast palette of Preferences › Accessibility (high_contrast.rs) is opaque too, and gives buttons, fields, windows, islands and dividers a light border: a new border goes through widgets::border_stroke so that it follows. Outside a window, a surface is drawn with widgets::overlay. An already positioned scene label uses widgets::paint_world_label, with a lighter tint and shaded scene blur to keep its text readable. With no world behind, as on the login screen, use widgets::overlay_frame. Never write a background, border or radius by hand. The bars keep the bar colour as their tint (widgets::finish_bar_glass); the minimap keeps the Legacy Viewer’s opacity with the border and the shadow of the windows. Menus, submenus, right-click menus and drop-down lists get this glass from a single place (menu::glaze, at the end of the frame, in the world): a new menu has nothing to do to get it. Gradients are reserved for brand elements (login background, grid wash, fill of the progress bar).
  • Preferences › Block. The page of what Block does differently from the Legacy Viewer carries the brand in two places only: its entry in the category list (edge mark and name in the brand gradient, from success through teal, indigo_light and violet_light to rose) and the head of the page (signal/data wash over the navy sky, a hairline of the same gradient along its bottom edge). The settings below keep the cards and controls of the other pages; the choices between Block’s look and the Legacy Viewer’s are a widgets::segmented track, Block’s segment lit in the accent and the Legacy Viewer’s in raised. It is not a precedent for the other pages (human’s choice, 2026-10-14).
  • Login screen. It is the viewer’s showcase. The window blur only knows the 3D scene, which is absent here: each card (login::card) therefore redraws under its tint the sky it covers, out of focus (block_bg::frosted: no stars, soft brand blooms, modular grid softened, ridges feathered), with signal and data glows that spill around it. Its top edge catches the light; the login card carries the hairline with the brand gradient. The news sits next to the login card, on its row and at its height (the list scrolls), the version above it. The bottom buttons and the version number share the same line.
  • Progress bars. widgets::progress: a flat track (that of the sliders) filled with the brand gradient, which slides to its value instead of jumping; the percentage written below slides with it. The loading and teleport banners slide in the same way. An instantaneous level (microphone) goes through widgets::level, which only smooths the jitter between two measurements. Never egui::ProgressBar.
  • Microphone button and panel. The microphone stays in the centre of its button, like the neighbouring icons; the arrow that follows it points to the panel it opens above the bar. The panel (window glass) gives the state of the voice, the two ways of talking as cards (the current one lit like an active button of the bar and ticked, the key to hold drawn as a key), then the microphone and the audio output one under the other, and the access to the preferences.
  • Loading and teleport. A “cinema banner” without a panel (ui/loading.rs): the last view, blurred, fills the whole screen, the name of the region is written large at the bottom left with what is known about it (maturity rating, arrival point, residents present) and the current step; at the bottom right, the tile of the region on the world map with the arrival point, and the progress in figures; the progress itself is a luminous hairline with the brand gradient along the whole bottom edge. The brand (wolf and name) stays discreet at the top left.
  • Discreet rounding. Flat design, almost square corners. Buttons: radius 5; windows, cards and pills of the toolbar: radius 3, with corners close to those of the build tools, including when collapsed. The radii come from the theme, never from a number in the code: ui.visuals().window_corner_radius for cards, pills, list highlights and image or colour frames, ui.visuals().widgets.inactive.corner_radius for buttons, widgets::inner_radius (one pixel less than the windows) for a small mark inside a control or a row: knob of a switch, key, status square, maturity rating letter. Left as they are: the deliberate right angles (radius 0), the circles (status dots, notification dot) and the thin bars (track of a progress bar or of a level, marker on a rail), whose 2 px ends come from a single constant, widgets::TRACK_RADIUS.
  • Name plate. The tag above an avatar (ui/nameplate.rs) is the only exception to the almost square corners: a glass plate with fully rounded ends. It is not a precedent: nothing else in the interface is rounder, but for the residents’ pictures (below).
  • Residents’ pictures. The profile pictures of the residents are round: on the name plates, and in the Conversations window (bubbles, head of a conversation, list), drawn by widgets::avatar_disc, with their status dot on the edge (ui/presence.rs). Only these pictures and the name plates are round; groups, places and objects keep square tiles. The bubbles of the conversations themselves keep the buttons’ radius, and the user’s own bubbles carry the accent gradient (violet to indigo), the one gradient outside the brand elements, chosen by the human (2026-10-14).
  • Glass: the tint of the plate (halfway between navy and bar, at 52%) over the window blur, a light one-pixel border (ink at 14%), a soft shadow below. The blur goes to the 16 nearest plates; the others, and the distant pills, only have the tint, denser. An opaque skin (panel_alpha = 255) has no blur.
  • Three levels depending on the distance to the camera: up to 15 m, the full plate (profile picture in a circle, status dot, display name in semi-bold, user name in muted, group title in teal, round badges floating on the top right edge); up to 40 m, a compact pill (name and badges on one line); beyond, the name alone. A plate only changes level 1.5 m beyond a threshold and slides from one level to the other in 0.18 s. In a crowd, only the eight nearest plates are full, the next ones are compact pills. A line hidden by the preferences is absent: never an empty line.
  • Status: dot at the bottom right of the picture, success (present), amber (away), danger (do not disturb); none for a blocked resident.
  • Badges: success microphone (in the voice channel), three animated dots (typing), success heart (friend), amber triangle (avatar too complex, shown as a silhouette), danger no-entry sign (blocked).
  • Voice: in the voice channel, the outline of the plate takes the success colour, at every level; while the person is speaking, a tight halo around the plate follows the level of their voice (fast rise, slower fall, no halo in silence).
  • Name colour. Every display name in ink, ours too; a blocked resident’s in italics on a plate at 62%. Preferences › Colors › “Color the names by relation” (off by default) colours the names of the plates and of the classic name tags alike, with the colours of that page (me, friends, blocked, Linden Lab, from the palette by default).
  • Size: the display name keeps the size of the classic name tags (13 points) on the full plate; everything else follows from it through the proportions of the mock-up.
  • Solid buttons. All buttons and drop-down lists are solid faces without a visible border: the theme (theme.rs) gives their border the colour of the face, only input fields and checkboxes keep an outline. A button therefore sets neither a background nor a radius itself, except for the main action (violet) or a destructive one (danger). An action with an icon uses widgets::action_button. The bottom toolbar has no separator lines: the spacing and the pill of the hovered or active button are enough.
  • Switches and checkboxes. The switch (widgets::switch) is almost square: track with the theme radius, square knob that slides. It is used for a standalone setting (alone with its label: widgets::switch_labeled); lists of flags (rights, data of a report, cells of a table) keep checkboxes, drawn by widgets::checkbox (flat square, violet and ticked when it is active), never by ui.checkbox.
  • Radio buttons. A choice among several goes through widgets::radio (radio_value for a value), never through ui.radio: the row of the checkbox (same size, same label, same hover), but round so that it is not taken for a checkbox: thin ring at rest, violet around a dot when the choice is made. It is the only round control of the interface; the other circles are marks (status dots, notification dot).
  • Collapsible sections. widgets::collapsing: a full-width row with a Tabler chevron and the title of the section, lit as a pill on hover, without a line; never egui::CollapsingHeader.
  • Colours. A colour is chosen with widgets::color_button: the swatch opens a small glass panel (saturation / value field, hue bar, new colour above the old one, R G B and hexadecimal fields, eyedropper), never egui’s color_edit_button_*. The colour palette of the build tools stays a separate window.
  • Scroll bars. Thin and solid, in a strip of their own at the right of what scrolls, faint at rest: the theme sets them for every scroll area (widgets::reserved_scroll_style). Never egui’s floating bar, which covers the switches, buttons and values of the right edge; a window does not set its own scroll style.
  • Shared hierarchy. Window title and controls at the top, stable navigation, content grouped by function and accessible actions. The inventory keeps a large list and a single status line at the bottom, without a details sheet for the selection. Lists scroll within their area and do not make the window grow. The build tools use a compact inspector with a rail of sections.
  • Light navigation. The tabs and navigation controls of the inventory are underlined in grey at rest, in violet when activated, without an outline. Hover stays discreet. The secondary actions of dialogs keep their dark surface. The command bar directly follows the title; the clear cross is built into the filter, without moving the controls.
  • A single accent colour per area. Violet signals what is active or important; the rest is neutral.
  • Contrast and readability. ink text on panel; secondary text in muted. Never dark violet text on a dark background (use violet_light). On a solid violet face, text and icons in on_accent (neither hard-coded white, nor ink, which a skin can darken); on a danger or amber face, which are light, the text stays in navy. Signed height sliders use widgets::height_slider: a visible neutral track, zero marker, violet fill from zero and a solid rectangular handle in the theme’s radius. Secondary controls can be grey while retaining their interaction; the hover-height manual controls switch automatic mode off when edited. The surrounding shared floater keeps its glass.
  • Very subtle but visible separators. A thin line of a neutral barely lighter than the background, so that one can see where each element starts and ends without weighing things down. Between two areas of a window: widgets::divider (horizontal in a column, vertical in a row); between two groups of entries of a menu: menu::separator; a line placed by hand (edge of a rail, boundary between two panes) takes widgets::divider_stroke. Never ui.separator(), and no line between buttons or the elements of a toolbar.
  • Typography. Inter (regular, semi-bold) by default; Noto Sans, Roboto and OpenDyslexic (an accessibility choice for readers with dyslexia), each with its own semi-bold or bold face, as the other embedded choices of Preferences › Fonts. No other font without a reason. Every text follows the chosen font: semi-bold through fonts::semibold, monospace only for code-like text (script editor, UUIDs, keys, colour codes, URLs, paths, versions), and an arrow or a caret is a Tabler icon, never a character (widgets::disclosure_caret, sort_caret). The characters they lack (display names and messages in Georgian, Japanese, Arabic, mathematical letters…) are drawn with the system fonts, which are never chosen for a text of the interface itself.
  • Text size. Preferences › Fonts and the skin’s font scale apply to all font faces, including explicit rich text, painter fonts and monospace. Keep font sizes in unscaled points; shaping supplies the scaled advances and row heights. Size custom text containers from their laid-out text.
  • Regular spacing. Reuse the existing widgets (ui/widgets.rs: flat buttons, switches, help badges, warning triangle…) rather than recreating them.
  • Interface texts are short and clear, in English and French through block_i18n::tr! (see TRANSLATING.md); English is the fallback. A tooltip explains every option whose effect is not obvious.
  • Snapping windows. A window moved near another one, a bar or an edge follows the pointer without a jump. Moving without Alt creates no snap or attachment. With Alt held, what will happen on release is shown by a guide: a 2 px line in violet_light along the edges that are going to touch (over the length they share) or to align, and the very light outline of the place where the window will land, with the window radius. No guide, no snapping. Snapped windows touch, edge to edge, with each other as with the bars and the edges, with no gap, like those of the first-launch layout (layout::GAP, at 0). During the gesture, a small pill on the window glass recalls Alt’s role: the keycap (widgets::keycap) followed by “snap and attach” for a free move, “detach this window” for a grouped move, or “do not snap” for a snapped resize. Alt detaches a moved window from its group and enables attachment to a new target; resizing keeps automatic snapping, disabled by Alt. The pill uses muted text, placed near the window, away from the guide and the pointer, after a short delay. No button and no permanent decoration for snapped windows.

README buttons

The buttons of the README header and its “Soon” pill are small SVG files in docs/readme/, drawn like the viewer’s own: flat face, radius of 4, a Tabler icon and Inter SemiBold.

The text is live text, not outlines. Its textLength is the width of the label in Inter SemiBold at the file’s font size, so that the layout holds with the fallback fonts of a machine without Inter. For another button, copy a file, change the icon paths, the label and that width, then the width of the svg and of its rect (18 + 20 + 9 + text + 18).

Icons: Tabler

All the icons of the interface are Tabler Icons (MIT), drawn in white then tinted with the palette. Do not mix them with another icon set.

  • The full library (version 3.49.0) is in assets/tabler-icons/: outline/ (5,184 outline icons) and filled/ (1,054 filled icons). This is where an icon is chosen; the Tabler site lets you search by keyword.
  • Only the icons in use are embedded in the viewer: crates/block-viewer/assets/icons/tabler/outline/<name>.svg and …/tabler/filled/<name>.svg.
  • In the code, an icon bears its Tabler name: eye-off for the outline version, eye-filled for the filled version.

The filled version first. Tabler only offers a filled version for one icon in five: we take it whenever it exists and expresses the function well. The outline version is used in three cases:

  • linear glyphs, which have no surface to fill: cross, plus, minus, tick, arrows, undo / redo, refresh, rotate, </>;
  • struck-through icons (eye-off, microphone-off, volume-off, message-off, ban for blocking): Tabler only draws them as outlines;
  • functions with no suitable filled icon: object (box), people in the plural (users, users-group, user-plus), loudspeaker (volume), map (map), hands (hand-grab, hand-stop), sitting (armchair), walking / running (walk, run)…

Do not force a filled icon that does not express the function. Two icons that go as a pair (link / unlink, plus / minus) stay in the same style. A function keeps the same icon everywhere (script = code, object = box, outfit = hanger-2-filled, profile = user-filled, block = ban, a friend’s right to modify our objects = pencil-filled…): look for the icon already in use before adding one.

Adding an icon:

  1. Choose the name in assets/tabler-icons/filled/, otherwise in assets/tabler-icons/outline/ (e.g. microphone.svg).
  2. Copy it into crates/block-viewer/assets/icons/tabler/filled/ (or outline/).
  3. Add its name to the FILLED (or OUTLINE) list of crates/block-viewer/src/ui/icons.rs.
  4. Use it with icons.get("microphone-filled") (or icons.get("volume") for an outline icon), tinted by the palette.

The icons of the parcel permissions (right end of the location field) are coloured green / red; a bar drawn by the code strikes through those that are forbidden. The icon in front of the web links of the chat and of the profiles is circle-check-filled in success (trusted site), alert-triangle-filled in amber (external link) or circle-x-filled in danger (dangerous link), also in their warnings.

Cursors

The world cursors are drawn from the same Tabler outline icons, in the ink colour outlined with bar to stay readable over any scenery (crates/block-viewer/src/cursors.rs). The rules are those of the Legacy Viewer, only the drawings change:

Situation Cursor
Object to touch hand-finger alone, the fingertip is the clicked point
Sit, buy, pay, open arrow + badge armchair, shopping-cart, coin, package
Media: play, pause, open arrow + badge player-play, player-pause, device-tv
Zoom on the object arrow + badge zoom-in
Grab a physical object, move a HUD hand-grab alone
Camera: Alt, Ctrl + Alt, Ctrl + Alt + Shift focus-centered, view-360, arrows-move, centred on the targeted point
Eyedropper of the colour picker color-picker, the tip is the sampled point

With the arrow + badge, the tip of the arrow is the clicked point.

Shortcut keys

In Preferences › Shortcuts, each key of a shortcut is a light capsule on the dark button, without a “+” between them. Letters, digits, F1 to F9 and the arrows use Tabler’s key icons (square-letter-w-filled, square-number-1-filled, square-f5-filled, square-arrow-up-filled). The other keys (Ctrl, Alt, Shift, Esc, F10 to F12, numeric keypad, accented letters…) are a capsule of the same shape around their name; a mouse button adds the mouse-filled icon before its name, as Tabler has no icon per button.
