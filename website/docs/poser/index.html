For testers

Local photo poser

Local trial for issue #727, based on the merged IK workbench (PR #893) and the supplied Poser handoff and mockup. Issue #727 remains open while grid validation is pending. The human validated the local demo on 2026-10-23 and authorized main integration on 2026-10-24. Two-client grid validation remains required.

The poser edits joint rotations and scales directly and moves joints through their articulated ancestor chains. It reuses the shaped Bento skeleton and transform tools from the IK editor; it does not evaluate IK projects or create bone-to-prim/bone bindings. Its selection, overlays and pose history are independent of the binding editor and virtual controls.

Posing

Open Build > Poser, or add Poser to the toolbar. In the photo mode’s viewfinder, which hides the rest of the interface, the dock’s wand button opens it over the shot (#1389): its joints take their clicks before the focus, and a worn HUD keeps its own. The window follows the layout validated on 2026-10-24: the subject strip (subject, posing status, “Visible to you only” with the full notice as tooltip, Start / Stop), the tabs, the joints column on the left and the selected joint on the right, then the file footer. The subject button opens a menu with yourself and your owned animesh roots, then every nearby avatar with its consent action (see Consent); Privacy › “Who can ask to pose my avatar” is its last entry. Other subjects must be within 50 m, in the same region and unmuted. Several subjects can retain independent poses at once. Eligibility is checked every frame; losing it removes the corresponding local pose.

The pose captures the displayed shaped skeleton and overrides its final animation palette. Body, Face, Hands, Other bones and Physics expose the actual Bento joints and collision volumes. Whole-avatar movement is the first Body row. Each joint pair has one row: the Side selector above the list (Left / Right / Mirror) chooses which one is edited, and Mirror is the symmetry (the left joint is edited and its counterpart follows). A joint picked in the world shows its side in the selector. The Hands tab has the five presets, applied to the chosen side (both in Mirror, in one undo step), and a finger grid: finger × base, middle and tip. Edited joints have violet dots, including their in-world markers. Only the active tab’s joints and shafts are visible and selectable. Changing tabs selects a joint in that group and replaces the previous group’s gizmo.

Use the Rotation / Position / Scale inspector, rotation trackball, axis sliders or in-world movement/rotation/scale gizmos. The trackball’s axes and drag directions follow the current camera, including orbit and roll. Horizontal and vertical trackball drags rotate the joint with the pointer. Rotations use the joint’s parent frame in degrees; positions use avatar axes in metres; scales are factors. Delta mode is relative to the captured initial pose. Children follow their parent with the normal skeleton scale rules. Parent offsets are retained during positional edits: moving a wrist bends its shoulder/elbow chain, without detaching or stretching the hand away from the forearm. A target beyond the chain’s reach is clamped. Only the unparented skeleton root translates freely. Explicit scale edits still change proportions through the normal skeleton rules. A world lock attempts to hold a joint’s position and orientation when its ancestors or subject move, yielding at reach limits to preserve attachments. Scale has local X/Y/Z cube handles and a central uniform-scale cube. Drag an axis cube outward to enlarge that channel, or drag the central cube right/up to enlarge all three channels. Scale values stay bounded and keep their sign: some shape sliders mirror a face or pectoral joint on one axis (Head Shape at 100 does it to the forehead), and the poser keeps that axis. Dragging a joint marker directly rotates or moves that joint, according to the selected transform mode. This changes the pose without assigning a target. Loaded pose/animation translations also retain the captured shaped offsets; non-root displacement is retargeted through joint rotations.

The joint’s header holds its own actions: the world pin (lit while locked), Mirror (copies the joint to its counterpart) and Reset joint. The Whole pose group holds Flip, Left → right, Right → left, T-pose and Reset all. Mirror on the Side selector mirrors ongoing edits. The hand presets Relaxed, Fist, Open, Point and Pinch follow the Side selector. Undo/redo is independent per subject and retains 64 checkpoints; a continuous drag creates one checkpoint. The title bar’s curved arrows undo and redo, as do Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y (or Ctrl+Shift+Z), unless a text field has the keyboard or the build tools’ window is in front of the poser’s (their Undo then). Gestures bound to these keys wait while the poser is open. Reset joint / Reset all restore the captured initial channels. T-pose restores the rig’s neutral rotations.

Stop releases a subject’s pose while retaining its edits for Start. Closing stops posing by default; disable that setting to keep poses with the window closed. Switching to IK editing, teleporting or logging out releases the poser. Poses affect only this viewer, including poses of other residents.

Library and files

Save writes a named *.block-pose.json under the current account’s local poses/ directory. It retains rotation, relative translation, scale and world-lock flags, without subject IDs, avatar names or world coordinates. The book in the title bar shows the library in the joints column’s place (the window keeps its size): back arrow, refresh, open folder, search, the entries with their date; a double click loads everything. Save’s menu (its chevron) holds the optional BVH output next to the pose file, the pelvis unlock and the priority. Load’s menu holds the four modes, applied to the selected library entry while the library is shown or to a file chosen otherwise, and “Open a file”.

Load accepts Block pose documents, native .anim files and .bvh files, up to 4 MiB. Animation imports sample their first frame. The four load modes are All, Rotations only, Unedited joints and Selective rotations. Selective loads preserve joints already edited in the current pose. Loading finishes on the subject originally chosen, even if the picker changes during I/O. World locks are reanchored to the destination subject.

Files and uploads contain what you authored, as in the Legacy Viewer (FSPoserAnimator::getJointExportRotation, FSFloaterPoser::savePoseToXml): the pose captured when posing starts is what the changes apply to, and stays out of them. T-pose clears it, so a pose built from there is saved complete; a hand preset or a loaded file does the same for its joints. Reset brings the captured pose back, and undo restores this state with the pose. A pose document stores complete joints as they are and the others as changes, which apply to the destination’s own pose (the Legacy Viewer’s “Save Diff”). Files saved before this are read as complete. BVH and uploads write the same changes on the rest skeleton: an untouched capture exports a T-pose.

BVH export writes the actual skeleton hierarchy in inches and Y-up, with a reference frame and two identical posed frames. The pelvis-unlock setting controls root rotation and translation. Native animations and BVH have no scale tracks; BVH also omits collision volumes. The Block pose document retains those channels. Upload as animation… reuses the existing native encoder and opens the normal import preview. Upload and the simulator price confirmation still require the human’s action; saving never uploads.

Consent

The subject menu lists every nearby avatar without probing their viewer or bridge. Ask needs the worn bridge (the request travels through its query service): until that service answers the button is disabled and the menu says why: the bridge is starting, it was taken off, it failed, or it is worn but its query script stays silent for 10 seconds. The last two offer Repair Block bridge, the Movelock window’s Repair. Ask sends one explicit request, with Cancel while pending. The target sees the requester, distance, the local-only photo use, the session lifetime, Refuse, Accept and a permanent-request-block checkbox. Accepted targets keep a reminder with Withdraw even when Poser is closed.

Privacy > Who can ask to pose my avatar defaults to Friends; Everyone and Nobody are also available. Friendship permits a request, never posing without acceptance. Muted or blocked requesters do not get a modal. No answer after 60 seconds expires the request; refusal and timeout impose a 10-minute cooldown. Responses require the matching request ID and peer. Withdrawal, disappearance, region changes, teleport and logout invalidate the relevant access. The requester immediately drops an invalid pose and receives a notice.

The bridge is version 1.34, which triggers the existing reprovisioning flow. The new service lives in query.lsl, separately from the movement controller. HTTPS commands use the existing validated query endpoint and token. Attachment-to-attachment traffic uses llRegionSayTo, request IDs and action/target fields only. The receiving script authenticates the sender with llGetOwnerKey and OBJECT_ATTACHED_POINT; the viewer additionally checks the worn bridge root, owner and region before consuming the private owner-say tag. There are no IMs, software advertisements or capability probes.

Settings and validation

The gear of the title bar opens the settings as a panel over the joint, grouped Window (stop on close, confirm a close that loses the pose, DoF while posing), Handling (markers, natural alignment, trackball sensitivity) and Files (overwrite confirmation, BVH, pelvis unlock, priority), with the note on the formats’ limits.

With stop on close, closing the window (its cross, Ctrl+W, the photo viewfinder’s wand) loses the edited joints of every subject. When some are edited, a dialog asks first: Minimize keeps the pose in the window’s bar, Close anyway closes, Escape or a click beside it cancels; “Don’t ask again” turns the setting off. The Legacy Viewer closes at once (FSFloaterPoser::onClose); Block asks so that no pose is lost by mistake.

Defaults were checked against the local Legacy Viewer source: stop on close, joint markers and natural alignment on; DoF in edit, additional BVH, pelvis unlock and overwrite confirmation off; trackball sensitivity 0.5. DoF while posing permits the normal configured DoF effect, without enabling it globally. Settings and privacy are saved through Block’s existing settings store.

Main integration validation on 2026-10-24: the script parser, formatting and Clippy pass; the complete workspace has 4,860 passing tests, zero failures and 29 ignored tests. The suite used one test thread on the shared PC. Twenty-three poser/consent regressions cover actual pointer actions for both hands, visible scroll clips, scaled text and window stability, pose/BVH/native files, eligibility, history and authenticated consent lifetime. Rotation and articulated movement drags create one undo step; idle controls preserve exact values, and stale IK bindings/virtual controls cannot affect the poser. Camera orbit/roll is checked against projected axes; pointer tests cover axis/uniform scale drags and tab-exclusive marker sets. Seventeen native poser scenarios and a separate IK bindings regression were inspected, with zero runtime errors. This does not validate real two-client LSL delivery.

Offline demos use AURORA_DEMO=1 and AURORA_DEMO_POSER= with self, connected, camera, scale, hands, face, misc, physics, library, settings, animesh, consent-accept, consent-refuse, consent-timeout, consent-withdraw, consent-request or consent-reminder. Capture frame 620. The synthetic request/answer events exercise the real consent state machine; they are not a substitute for two-client LSL transport validation.

Human checks: edit each tab and channel, use presets and undo, save/reload each load mode, enable BVH and inspect its import, confirm overwrite, prepare an animation import, switch subjects, and test both close behaviours. On a grid, use two accounts running this build to verify bridge reprovisioning, request/accept/refuse/withdraw, mute/privacy, timeout, region departure and logout. Also validate owned and unowned ground/worn animesh and mesh/shape overrides. The agent never logs into a grid or performs an upload.
